NeighborhoodShare: activation without retention, and the rebuild that followed

tool-sharing platform wound down, rebuilt 170 users across 20 communities rebuild: Supabase + Cloudflare Workers

Read this as a diagnosis I trust and a second version I have not proven.

The problem

I built NeighborhoodShare twice. The first version, on my own site: 170 users across 20 communities before I wound it down, because tool sharing proved too infrequent to stay top of mind (site/src/lib/content.ts:173). The rebuild is the same product, with tool lending as the whole scope. Borrowing a tool is something you do every six months, maybe, and a loop that runs twice a year has no second visit.

What was actually blocking use

The legacy numbers come from a technical document (content-drafts/neighborhoodshare-technical-doc.md). They separate into four failures I treated as one in 2025.

Acquisition worked. Roughly 60 signups came from door-to-door flyers and a neighborhood association meeting in my own zip, 95126. Roughly 110 more came from a Resilient Tomorrow newsletter post and email list.

Listing failed. About 75 to 80 tools sat in the production database, all but about 5 of them ones I added myself. Only 5 of the 95 active users ever listed anything, about 6 percent. The friction I was proud of removing was not the friction that stopped them.

Density never formed, and my rollout design was part of why. A zip only activated at 20 or more users plus a captain, with an adjacent-zip exception of 18 plus 2 within 5 miles. Twenty zips were active and 82 people were waiting.

Retention failed for a reason no interface change would fix. People did not come back, because a drill is not a daily or weekly need. Concentration alone did not rescue it: 95126, the one dense neighborhood, is where those 60 signups were, and listing was rare there.

What I designed

DEC-003 in PROJECT-MEMORY.json is the decision that matters for growth: rollout became open signup plus address and radius matching, and ZIP gating and the captain program were dropped.

Wanted posts let demand appear before supply. A neighbor posts what they need, any neighbor can hit I can help, which notifies the poster (migration 20260712080000_m8_trust_safety.sql).

Photo-first listing: /listings/new opens with one button, Add a photo to get started, creating a draft and jumping to AI autofill. The manual form stays behind a toggle (TASKS.md M12).

Points come only from completed exchanges, 25 for a lend and 10 for a borrow, clearing 24 hours later (migration 20260712070000_m7_gamification.sql). Precise address stays private, contact details hidden until approval.

What I'd change

The rebuild fixes the activation and supply problems I could name. It does not fix frequency, and I should not dress it up as if it does. The app at neighborhoodshare.app still asks people to borrow a drill.

I would pick one neighborhood and stay there: seed one radius with roughly 30 to 50 people recruited the way I recruited in 95126, and resist channels outside it.

I would instrument before the first signup. The relaunch epic (issue #3) defines the success measures, and I have measured none. The legacy beta ran PostHog and Plausible; the rebuild has no analytics dependency.

I would finish the referral path (issue #11) first, because density from existing members does not depend on behavioral frequency. My earlier write-up said tool sharing belongs inside a broader community product. I did not take that route, and I would rather say so than let a scope change I never made argue for me.

Growth read

The legacy beta is activation without retention, caused by behavioral frequency rather than product quality. Signups were cheap, listing was rare, and 82 people sat behind a gate requiring density nobody had.

The metric that matters is listings and completed exchanges per radius per unit of time, not signups.

The rebuild bets that activation and supply can be fixed and made visible. That bet is untested: no founding neighborhood has been recruited, the funnel is uninstrumented, and the referral lever is unshipped. Read this as a diagnosis I trust and a second version I have not proven.
