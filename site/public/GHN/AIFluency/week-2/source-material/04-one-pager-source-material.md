## Meeting Notes — Atlas Workshop Series (July 22 – August 28, 2026)

### Workshop 1: Operations — Margins under pressure (July 22)

**Attendees:** James (Strategy), Priya (COO), David (VP Ops Excellence), Marcus (Sr. Director, Network Design), Sandra (Sr. Director, Driver Ops)

**Key data points discussed:**
- Net margin dropped from 6.8% (2023) → 4.1% (2025). Driver wages up 22% over the period. Fuel costs up 14%. Customer pricing flat.
- 30% of routes are running below break-even — mostly in 8 of the 47 distribution zones (Chicago, Atlanta, Dallas, Phoenix, Denver, Newark, Charlotte, Seattle)
- We have the data to identify the worst routes; we don't have a process to act on it

**Decisions made:**
- Don't rebuild the network. That's a 3-year, $80M play.
- Focus on the 8 loss-making zones. Closing or restructuring them could save $14M/year.

**Open questions:**
- What's the political cost of closing zones that employ 340 people?
- Can we restructure routes within zones rather than close them?

**Action items:**
- Priya: Build the per-zone P&L view by Aug 12
- Marcus: Model 3 restructuring scenarios (close, restructure, hybrid) by Aug 26
- James: Bring the options to the next workshop

### Workshop 2: Technology — AI is changing the competitive landscape (Aug 5)

**Attendees:** James (Strategy), Priya (COO), Lin (CTO), Rachel (VP Data), Tom (Director, Customer Tech)

**Key data points discussed:**
- Three of our top 5 competitors are piloting AI-powered route optimization. Published case studies suggest 8–15% fuel and labor savings.
- Helio's current routing system is 7 years old. Last major update was 2019. Integration with new AI vendors would take 12–18 months.
- Our data is good (95% GPS coverage, 4 years of route history). Our infrastructure is not.
- A separate issue: customers are asking about AI-driven ETA predictions. We're 6 months behind competitors on basic features.

**Decisions made:**
- Don't build AI in-house. Buy.
- We need to pick a vendor and commit within 6 months, or we'll lose 2–3 enterprise accounts.

**Open questions:**
- Are we buying a tool, or are we buying a transformation partner?
- What's the org change required to actually use the tool?

**Action items:**
- Lin: Shortlist 3 vendors by Aug 19. Get pricing.
- Rachel: Define what "good" looks like — KPI baseline, adoption metrics, ROI criteria.
- Tom: Talk to 3 customers about what they actually want from AI features.

### Workshop 3: Sales — The enterprise pipeline is shifting (Aug 19)

**Attendees:** James (Strategy), Keisha (CRO), Brian (VP Enterprise Sales), Aisha (VP Mid-Market), Daniel (Sales Ops)

**Key data points discussed:**
- Enterprise pipeline grew 38% YoY but win rate dropped from 31% → 24%. We have more deals, we close fewer of them.
- Lost-deal reasons (per last quarter): "Missing AI/tech capabilities" (cited in 41% of losses), "Pricing" (18%), "Service concerns" (12%), "Other" (29%)
- Average sales cycle for enterprise has stretched from 78 days → 112 days. Buyers are doing more diligence.
- Mid-market pipeline is flat. SMB is declining 8% YoY.

**Decisions made:**
- The enterprise motion is the growth vector. Mid-market and SMB are mature/declining.
- We need a different GTM for enterprise — solution selling, not product selling.

**Open questions:**
- Are we organized to sell enterprise? (Current org is built for mid-market.)
- What's our defensibility against AI-native competitors entering the mid-market?

**Action items:**
- Keisha: Propose enterprise GTM redesign by Sept 5.
- Daniel: Quantify the lost-deal-attrition cost vs. investment in capabilities.
- Aisha: Test an enterprise pilot motion with 5 named accounts in Q4.

### Workshop 4: HR — Talent is the constraint (Aug 28)

**Attendees:** James (Strategy), Priya (COO), Lin (CTO), Sandra (Sr. Director, Driver Ops), Yvonne (CHRO), Marcus (Sr. Director, Network Design)

**Key data points discussed:**
- Driver turnover: 78% annualized. Industry average: 60%. Replacing a driver costs ~$4,500.
- Tech talent: 23 open reqs, average time-to-fill 142 days. Three offers declined in the last month for competitors with stronger AI/tech narratives.
- AI-fluency gap: of the 4,200 employees, fewer than 80 can credibly use AI tools in their work today. We've piloted AI training with two teams; both saw 20%+ productivity gains.
- The board has historically not funded training. This is the first time we're putting it in a strategy paper.

**Decisions made:**
- Talent is the binding constraint on every other initiative. We can't execute the AI play, the zone restructuring, or the enterprise GTM without it.
- Whatever we propose to the board, training has to be in it.

**Open questions:**
- What's the right level of investment? Where does the money go?
- How do we measure whether the training worked?

**Action items:**
- Yvonne: Build a 3-year talent investment plan by Sept 12. Cover drivers, tech, AI fluency.
- Sandra: Define what "good" looks like for driver retention.

## Three Proposed Initiatives (consolidated for the board)

### Initiative A: Zone Restructuring (from Workshop 1)

**The bet:** Restructuring 8 loss-making zones saves $14M/year within 12 months.

**Investment required:** $6M (consultants, transition costs, severance)
**Time to impact:** 12–18 months
**Confidence:** High — operational, well-understood problem
**Risk:** Political. 340 jobs affected. Requires careful communication.

### Initiative B: AI Capability Investment (from Workshops 2, 3, 4)

**The bet:** Buying AI tooling (route optimization, ETA prediction, customer-facing features) closes the competitive gap, recovers lost enterprise deals, and unlocks 8–15% operational savings.

**Investment required:** $22M over 3 years ($8M vendor, $6M integration, $8M training + org change)
**Time to impact:** 12 months for tooling, 24–36 months for full ROI
**Confidence:** Medium — depends on vendor choice and execution
**Risk:** If we pick the wrong vendor, we're stuck for 5+ years. If we don't train people, we own the tool but can't use it.

### Initiative C: Enterprise GTM Redesign (from Workshop 3)

**The bet:** Redesigning the sales motion around enterprise (solution selling, longer cycle, dedicated team) recovers win rate and grows the segment.

**Investment required:** $9M over 18 months (sales hiring, enablement, tools)
**Time to impact:** 6–9 months
**Confidence:** Medium — requires organizational change the company hasn't done before
**Risk:** If the AI/tech gap isn't closed in parallel, the new motion won't win anyway.

## The Decision the Board Needs to Make on Sept 30

**The core question:** Of the three initiatives, which one (or which combination) do we fund in 2027?

**The hidden tension (per James's private notes):** All three are interdependent. Zone restructuring can't happen without the operational data AI would provide. AI can't deliver ROI without trained people. The enterprise motion can't win without AI features. Talent is the binding constraint across all three.

**What the CEO wants from the board meeting:** A clear yes/no on each initiative, with a sequencing plan if more than one is approved. Not a "let's do all three" without prioritization.

**What the CEO does NOT want:** A 30-page strategic plan that requires the board to read between the lines.
