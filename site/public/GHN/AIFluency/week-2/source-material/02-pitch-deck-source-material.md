## One-Pager

**Company:** Pulseboard
**Stage:** Pre-seed
**Founder:** Marcus Rivera (first-time founder, ex-Stripe PM)
**Headcount:** 3 (founder + 2 engineers)
**Capital raised:** $180K (friends and family + a small angel)
**Runway:** 9 months at current burn

---

### Problem

Engineering leaders spend 4–6 hours per week in status meetings that produce zero written record. Decisions get made verbally, never captured, and onboarding new engineers takes 2–3x longer than it should because institutional context lives in people's heads and Slack history.

Mid-market engineering teams (50–500 engineers) feel this most acutely. They're too big for tribal knowledge to scale, too small to have a dedicated PMO.

### Solution

Pulseboard turns the conversations engineering leaders are already having into a searchable, structured knowledge base — automatically. The leader speaks for 10 minutes at the end of each day. Pulseboard transcribes, extracts decisions, action items, and context, and feeds them into a queryable system the team can search.

The founder's insight: the artifact already exists. Leaders are talking. Nobody's writing it down. Pulseboard is the writing.

### Market

- **TAM:** ~40,000 mid-market engineering organizations in North America
- **Pricing target:** $800/month per team (10–25 engineers)
- **Path to $10M ARR:** ~1,000 teams — achievable in 3 years at conservative growth

### Traction

- **6 design partners** signed (4–6 week pilots, free in exchange for feedback)
- **2 paying customers** at $400/month (early-bird rate, locked for 12 months)
- **Waitlist of 14** engineering leaders from the design partners' networks
- **NPS of 71** across design partner interviews
- **Qualitative signal:** "I cancelled two weekly meetings in the first week" (Head of Eng, fintech, 80 engineers)

### Ask

Raising a **$1.5M seed round** to:
- Hire 4 engineers (2 backend, 1 frontend, 1 ML)
- Extend runway from 9 months → 24 months
- Convert 6 design partners to paying customers
- Ship the public beta by month 6

Lead investor wanted. Comfortable with co-lead or follower.

## Three Investor Personas

Use these to triangulate what the deck should optimize for. Each persona will care about different things in different slides.

---

### Persona A: Former Operator Turned VC

**Name:** "Lin"
**Background:** Ex-VP Eng at a Series C SaaS company, angel-invested in 12 infra tools, joined a $40M seed fund 8 months ago.

**What she cares about:**
- Founder quality — has Marcus worked with engineers before? Can he ship?
- Is this a tool or a company? (Does the founder think 5 years out?)
- Honest assessment of what's hard (Lin has been through hard engineering org problems)
- Will intro to her portfolio companies if she's interested

**What makes her say no:**
- Founder who blames the market for slow progress
- Pitch that sounds like a feature list, not a company thesis
- Asking for $1.5M without a clear plan for what happens at month 6

**What she wants to see:**
- A founder who's thought harder about the problem than the pitch deck
- Conviction that's backed by customer signal, not vibes

---

### Persona B: Pre-Seed Specialist

**Name:** "Devon"
**Background:** Runs a pre-seed fund ($250K-$750K checks). Invests in 15–20 companies a year. Has seen hundreds of engineering tools.

**What he cares about:**
- Speed of insight — does Marcus know what he doesn't know?
- Founder-market fit
- A specific wedge: why this, why now, why Marcus?
- What happens if Pulseboard doesn't hit plan — what's the fallback?

**What makes him say no:**
- TAM that's clearly aspirational (the $10B AI transcription market, not the $1B engineering tools market)
- Founder who's overly polished on the surface and vague on the details
- No clear technical insight behind the product

**What he wants to see:**
- A real founder who's talked to 50+ potential customers
- Specific use cases with named customers
- A clear thesis on why this wins

---

### Persona C: Strategic Angel

**Name:** "Priya"
**Background:** Former CTO, now fractional CTO for 4 startups. Angel-invested in 30+ companies. Strong network in engineering leadership.

**What she cares about:**
- Does this solve a real problem she still feels?
- Is the founder coachable?
- Will she be able to help (and will the founder actually use her help)?

**What makes her say no:**
- Founder who doesn't ask her opinion
- Product that tries to do too much in v1
- Pricing that doesn't match the value delivered

**What she wants to see:**
- A specific story about a customer who's had an "aha" moment
- A clear answer to "what's the one thing you'd never change"
- A founder who'll keep her in the loop after she invests
